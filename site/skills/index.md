# Browser Skills

A Browser Skill is a reusable instruction you hand to your assistant. Teach it a multi-step task once and you can trigger the whole thing with a single request from then on, instead of spelling out the steps every time. A recurring job becomes something the assistant just knows how to do.

Some Skills are practical one-shots: show a QR code for the current page, save a page as a PDF, grab a YouTube transcript. Others wrap up a longer routine you run often. Either way the point is the same. Describe it once, reuse it forever.

::: tip Not the same as the phi-browser skill
**Browser Skills** are what this page is about: instructions you give the assistant inside Phi. The **[phi-browser skill](/phi-browser-skill/)** is a different thing entirely, a package that lets an outside AI agent such as Claude Code drive Phi from your terminal. Same word, two meanings, so we keep them apart: Browser Skills live in the browser, the phi-browser skill lives in your agent.
:::

## Built-in Skills and your own

There are two kinds:

- **Built-in Skills**, a set Phi provides and maintains, ready to use.
- **Your own Skills**, the ones you create for the way you work.

Built-in Skills are opt-in. They are there to browse, and you add the ones you want to your library rather than having everything switched on at once.

## The Skills library

The **Skills** page is where you manage all of this. Open it from the menu in the **top-right corner of the new tab**, the same menu that holds your recent conversations and widgets, and choose **Skills**.

It opens with a spotlight of featured built-in Skills, and below that you can browse the full set. Filter by **All**, **Built-in**, **User created**, or **Enabled**, or search by name. It is the closest thing to a gallery of what your assistant can do.

For any Skill you can:

- **Add** a built-in Skill to your library, and remove it later. You can always add it back.
- **Enable or disable** a Skill without removing it, so you control which ones are active.
- **View** a Skill's full details to see exactly what it does before you use it.

## Creating your own

You are not limited to the built-in set. Phi includes a **Skill Creator** that helps you design and save a new Skill from a description of the task, so making one is itself a Skill you can just ask for. Your own Skills are plain instructions in your own words, and you can edit or delete them at any time as your routines change.

## Using Skills in chat

Skills run through your assistant, not on their own. Once a Skill is enabled, the assistant can reach for it by itself when your request matches what it does. You can also bring one into a conversation yourself: pick it from the Skills list while you are writing a message, including from the [new tab's ask box](/new-tab/), when you want to be explicit about which one to use.

## A Skill can act, so give it the same thought you would give yourself

Skills can do more than fetch and summarise. A Skill that fills forms, posts, or works through a site is doing that as you, from your browser, with your sessions. Two things follow.

Anything with consequences is still yours: a purchase, a message sent, a form submitted. Read what a Skill does before you enable it, which is what the **View** detail is for.

And some sites do not welcome automation. Reddit is the well-known example, where accounts detected as automated get restricted or banned, and that call is theirs rather than ours. If a Skill works through a site like that, you are taking that risk with your account. See [the phi-browser skill](/phi-browser-skill/) for more on this.
